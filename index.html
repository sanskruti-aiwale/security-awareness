    Four modules, one habit each

    Open a module for the short version. Each one takes about two minutes.

    Phishing

    Spot fake messages before they work.
    • Check the sender. Look at the full address, not just the display name.
    • Hover before you click. The real destination shows at the bottom of your screen.
    • Distrust urgency. Deadlines and threats are built to stop you thinking.
    • Verify out of band. Call the person using a number you already have.

    Passwords

    Long, unique and backed by a second step.
    • Use a passphrase. Four or more random words beat a short, complex password.
    • Never reuse. One breach should never unlock your other accounts.
    • Use a password manager. It remembers so you don’t have to.
    • Turn on MFA. Prefer an authenticator app over text messages.

    Device safety

    Small habits that close big gaps.
    • Install updates. They patch holes attackers already know about.
    • Lock your screen. Every time you step away, and turn on device encryption.
    • Careful with public Wi-Fi. Use a VPN or your phone’s hotspot.
    • Skip unknown USB drives. A found drive is a classic trap.

    Responding

    What to do when something looks wrong.
    • Stop and don’t reply. Leave links and attachments alone.
    • Report it fast. Use your report button or contact IT right away.
    • Clicked already? Change the affected passwords and tell IT. There is no blame.

    Case study: one phone call at MGM Resorts

    In September 2023, public reports describe attackers getting into a major casino operator by calling its help desk, not by breaking its software.

    1. Research

      Attackers found an employee’s details online.

    2. The call

      They phoned the IT help desk and posed as that employee.

    3. The reset

      Access was reset without strong identity checks.

    4. The fallout

      Ransomware disrupted operations. MGM later reported about $100M in impact.

    Verify identity before every reset, with no exceptions.
