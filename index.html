    The field guide

    Phishing

    • Check the sender. Read the full address, not the display name.
    • Hover before you click. The real destination shows on screen.
    • Distrust urgency. Deadlines are built to stop you thinking.
    • Verify another way. Call a number you already have.

    Passwords

    • Mix it up. Combine capitals, lowercase letters, numbers and symbols.
    • Make it long. A passphrase of four random words beats a short, tricky one.
    • Never reuse. One breach shouldn’t open every account.
    • Use a manager. It remembers so you don’t.
    • Turn on MFA. An authenticator app beats text messages.

    Devices

    • Install updates. They close holes attackers already know.
    • Lock your screen whenever you step away, and encrypt the disk.
    • Be wary of public Wi-Fi. Use a VPN or your hotspot.
    • Skip unknown USB drives. Found drives are a classic trap.

    Responding

    • Stop, and don’t reply. Leave links and files alone.
    • Report fast. Use the report button or tell IT.
    • Already clicked? Change the passwords and say so. Nobody gets blamed.

    Password lab

    Type a practice password and see how long a fast attacker would need. Try a short one, then four random words.

      Case study: MGM Resorts

      In September 2023, public reports describe attackers getting into a major casino operator without breaking its software. They used a phone.

      1. Research

        Attackers found an employee’s details online.

      2. The call

        They phoned the IT help desk and posed as that employee.

      3. The reset

        Access was reset without strong identity checks.

      4. The fallout

        Ransomware disrupted operations. MGM later reported about $100M in impact.

      Verify identity before every reset, with no exceptions.
