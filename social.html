Social engineering

Phone, text and in-person tricks.

Why it matters

Attackers target people, not machines. A convincing story is cheaper than a hack, as the MGM case shows.

Scams also arrive by phone and text. Calls are called vishing and texts smishing.

They borrow trust. Posing as IT, a boss or a courier makes you drop your guard.

Verify before you trust.

The habits

  • Call back. Verify callers on a number you already have.
  • Never share codes. Real IT never needs your MFA code.
  • Mind the door. Don’t let strangers follow you into secure areas.
  • Leave found USB drives alone. Hand them to IT, unplugged.

Test yourself

Two options each time. Pick the better one.

Case study: MGM Resorts

In September 2023, public reports describe attackers getting into a major casino operator without breaking its software. They used a phone.

  1. Research

    Attackers found an employee’s details online.

  2. The call

    They phoned the IT help desk and posed as that employee.

  3. The reset

    Access was reset without strong identity checks.

  4. The fallout

    Ransomware disrupted operations. MGM later reported about $100M in impact.

Verify identity before every reset, with no exceptions.
