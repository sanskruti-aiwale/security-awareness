Why passwords matter

Attackers rarely guess one password at a time. They take lists of passwords leaked from other sites and try them everywhere. This is called credential stuffing, and it works whenever people reuse a password.

Weak passwords fall in moments. If a stolen password file is cracked offline, software can try billions of guesses a second. Short and predictable passwords are found first.

One account opens others. Your email can reset almost every other account you own, so losing it is rarely the end of the damage.

    The habits

    • Mix it up. Combine capitals, lowercase letters, numbers and symbols.
    • Make it long. A passphrase of four random words beats a short, tricky one.
    • Never reuse. One breach shouldn’t open every account.
    • Use a manager and turn on MFA. An authenticator app beats text messages.
